# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Australian homeowners considering rooftop solar, battery storage, or EV charging. Mostly families weighing a high-cost, infrequent purchase they don't fully understand. They arrive cost-anxious and skeptical (the solar market is full of cheap, pushy operators), researching on both desktop and mobile. The job: decide whether Kratos is trustworthy, understand what a system costs and saves, and request a quote.

## Product Purpose

Marketing site for Kratos Energy, a 100% Australian-owned solar company. It exists to convert researchers into qualified quote requests by establishing trust, explaining systems/savings/rebates plainly, and removing cost uncertainty. Success = quote-funnel completions and calculator engagement, backed by a credible, premium brand impression.

## Brand Personality

Trustworthy, local, expert. Calm confidence over hype. Australian-owned and credentials-forward (CEC accreditation, real case studies). Plain-spoken about money and savings, never pushy.

## Anti-references

- **Cheap door-to-door solar**: pushy, cluttered, neon-discount, spammy lead-gen.
- **Generic SaaS template**: cream backgrounds, tracked-uppercase eyebrows above every section, identical icon-card grids, gradient text.
- **Corporate/clinical utility**: cold gray, energy-bill-portal coldness.
- **Over-animated/flashy**: bouncy gimmicky motion that undercuts trust.

## Design Principles

1. **Earn trust before asking for the lead.** Credentials, proof, and clarity precede every CTA.
2. **Show the money plainly.** Costs, savings, and rebates stated in concrete numbers, not vague promises.
3. **Local and human, not corporate.** Real Australian families, installers, places; warmth carried by imagery and copy.
4. **Confident restraint.** Premium feel through composition and typography, not flash.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Broad consumer audience including older homeowners. Target WCAG AA: body contrast >=4.5:1, visible focus states, keyboard-navigable forms/calculators, reduced-motion alternatives. Mobile-first reachability for one-handed use.
